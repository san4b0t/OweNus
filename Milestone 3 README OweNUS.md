## **2Motivation**

As undergraduate students at the National University of Singapore (NUS), many of us have faced situations where financial flexibility becomes essential—whether it’s managing day-to-day expenses, covering unexpected costs, or funding a personal project. In such situations, borrowing money from friends often becomes a practical and convenient solution. While friend-to-friend lending is rooted in trust and mutual support, it can sometimes lead to misunderstandings, delayed repayments, or even strained relationships when agreements are not clearly defined or honored.

Existing tools like Splitwise help track shared expenses, but they have limitations—such as daily caps on the number of expenses that can be added, and a lack of features for managing structured lending agreements or tracking repayment timelines. They were not built for actual lending workflows or financial accountability between individuals.

To address these gaps, we propose a decentralized peer-to-peer (P2P) lending platform built on blockchain technology. Our solution preserves the trust and familiarity of borrowing from friends, while adding smart contracts for enforceable, transparent agreements. Additionally, we aim to go beyond expense tracking by integrating AI-powered insights—offering users personalized reminders, repayment predictions, spending habit analysis, and risk alerts to enhance trust and financial awareness.

Our platform empowers students to lend and borrow in a secure, transparent, and scalable manner—combining the warmth of a handshake deal with the accountability of a formal system.

## **3Aim**

Our primary goal is to revolutionize the way individuals and groups manage shared expenses and personal loans by building a decentralized peer-to-peer (P2P) lending and expense management platform powered by blockchain technology. Unlike traditional tools that offer only basic tracking functionality, our platform is designed to provide a holistic, trust-based financial ecosystem tailored to real-world social and financial dynamics.

At its core, the platform will allow users to seamlessly coordinate and split expenses within groups, monitor pending payments, and reduce friction in repayments. A unique feature will be our credibility score system, which evaluates users based on payment punctuality and consistency. This scoring mechanism not only incentivizes responsible financial behavior but also fosters greater trust and accountability among peers laying the groundwork for more dependable financial interactions.

To further enhance the user experience, the platform will go beyond expense tracking. It will include smart automation tools such as customizable deadlines and automated reminders, AI-driven expense prediction based on past behavior, and personalized financial insights that help users align their spending with income patterns. These intelligent features empower users to take proactive control of their finances, anticipate upcoming obligations, and plan smarter, ultimately reducing the cognitive burden and stress often associated with informal lending and shared money matters.

By combining the transparency and security of blockchain with user-centric design and intelligent automation, our platform aspires to redefine trust, efficiency, and accountability in everyday financial relationships.

_Split smarter. Settle faster. Stay accountable._

## **4Vision**

OweNus will be a powerful tool for users to manage shared finances effectively, offering deep visibility into group spending behaviours, recurring expense patterns, and payment promptness. It aims to simplify the process of splitting, tracking, and settling group expenses, from casual outings to complex recurring payments.

OweNus will offer users personalized insights into their group financial habits, and attempts to make forecasts on payment delays, contribution trends, and even estimate a user’s decentralized creditworthiness based on historical behaviour. By integrating intelligent analytics and smart reminders, it nudges users to act promptly and responsibly. Through quantitative metrics such as contribution ratios, settlement frequency, and expense timelines, OweNus motivates users to be more financially accountable while reducing friction in money matters between friends, peers, or communities.

OweNus ultimately helps users reach their goal of managing shared expenses with clarity and fairness through short financial cycles — empowering them to make better decisions, reduce social discomfort around money, and foster long-term financial transparency in their social circles

## **5User Stories**

1.  As a student, I can borrow small amounts of money through the platform with transparent terms, so that I don’t have to rely on informal borrowing or risk misunderstandings with friends.
2.  As a student, I can lend money to peers with a smart contract-based agreement, so that I have confidence I’ll be repaid on time.
3.  As a user, I can track all group expenses in one place, so that I don’t lose track of who owes what across multiple social settings.
4.  As a user, I can receive timely reminders about upcoming repayments, so that I can avoid late payments and maintain my credibility score.
5.  As a borrower, I can view my credibility score and see how it's affected by my repayment behavior, so that I understand how others view my reliability.
6.  As a user, I can view predictive analytics about my spending habits and upcoming obligations, so that I can make better financial decisions.
7.  As a group member, I can split both recurring and one-time expenses, so that our financial coordination remains seamless and fair.
8.  As a financially responsible student, I can build a public credibility history, so that I can eventually use it to access larger or more trusted loans.
9.  As a platform user, I can resolve disputes or clarify repayments using immutable records on the blockchain, so that misunderstandings don’t escalate into conflicts.

### **6Features**

#### **6.1Personalized Expense Splitter**

## The Personalized Expense Splitter is a core feature designed to automate and simplify shared expense management within groups. Users can split bills and shared costs using various models such as equal division, income-weighted proportions, or custom allocations. The system adapts to each group’s dynamics, removing the need for manual calculations and promoting fairness and transparency. Its intuitive interface ensures that users can quickly settle costs, whether for daily expenses, trips, or recurring group activities.

#### **6.2Informative Dashboard**

## The informative dashboard offers a real-time overview of recent transactions, group activities and outstanding expenses. The dashboard starts off with the user’s wallet balance that is taken from their connected wallet to the app. The top card of the dashboard also entails 3 most recent expenses and net balances owed from friends. With visually organised summaries and smart filters, users can instantly track their financial movements, stay updated on shared costs and monitor overall financial activity all in one place.

#### **6.3Authentication System**

## Secure login and identity verification opens the door to the multitude of features that OweNUS has to offer. We employ Firebase’s own in-house authentication for easier integration with our backend that runs on Firestore by Firebase. With this robust authentication mechanism, we ensure access is restricted to authorized users only and user data is therefore safeguarded. This ensures privacy, integrity and seamless user experience.

#### **6.4Payment Deadlines and Reminders**

## Users can set custom payment deadlines when splitting bills or lending funds. Automated reminders are sent via in-app alerts before due dates. This reduces the likelihood of forgotten payments, making the repayment process seamless and timely. With reduced chances of missed payments, this system ensures a smooth and punctual repayment flow between friends or group members. 

#### **6.5Expense Predictor**

## Powered by AI, the expense predictor uses past transaction history to forecast upcoming expenses based on how many people and how long the lender gives the deadline for repayment. It identifies behavioural trends in terms of punctuality in repayment to provide tailored financial projections for a possible expense to be logged. As the model accounts for latest repayments, users can be assured the predicted loss from an expense is accurate. 

#### **6.6Credit Scoring**

## Dynamic credit scoring that tracks and evaluates user payment behaviour over time. Factors such as punctuality and consistency contribute to the user’s real-time credit score that is reflected in the profile page. This score is displayed publicly to other friends when they want to add an expense. This feature fosters trust in peer-to-peer financial transactions. 

#### **6.7Friends feature**

## The Friends feature allows users to add, manage, and organize their network of financial connections. With friend tagging in expenses, shared transaction histories, and quick-split options, this feature enhances collaboration and makes settling group expenses more intuitive and personal.

#### **6.8Real-time MetaMask wallet connection** 

## Users can securely connect their MetaMask wallets to the platform and view real-time on-chain balances directly from the dashboard. This allows users to read their wallet balance from MetaMask directly from their dashboard on OweNUS. This seamless integration bridges OweNUS with the local cryptocurrency app on the user’s mobile device, ensuring transparency and instant access to personal financial resources.

#### **6.9Programmatic Fund Transfer via MetaMask Testnet**

## OweNUS supports programmatic fund transfers through MetaMask using testnet environments. This feature allows users to simulate real-world transfers securely, enabling smoother transactions and future scalability to mainnet environments when production-ready.

#### **6.10Equal/Custom Expense Creation** 

## The Smart Expense Creation feature allows users to add new expenses with ease, offering both equal and custom split options. When creating an expense, users can select participants from a visual list of friends, each displayed alongside their current credit score for added transparency and informed decision-making. A repayment deadline can also be set during creation, integrating seamlessly with reminders and the credit scoring system. This makes shared expenses clearer, fairer, and more accountable from the start.

#### **6.11Summary of features** 

## While current platforms like Splitwise limit the number of daily transactions, OweNUS is built for scalability from the ground up. We overcome these constraints by leveraging a decentralized architecture and real-time syncing. With the integration of AI-powered insights, OweNUS goes beyond simple expense tracking to become a full-fledged financial co-pilot for students and small communities alike.

## Each feature within OweNUS is intentionally designed to blend user-friendliness with financial intelligence, ensuring that shared expenses and personal lending are no longer sources of friction but catalysts for collaboration and trust.

**10.1.3 Sign In / Sign Up Features**

#### **Description**

Authentication in OweNus is powered by **Firebase Authentication**, and is essential for providing a personalized, secure, and user-specific experience across all financial features. Each user must sign up or log in using their **email and password**, after which they are granted access to features such as group expense tracking, smart insights, and personalized dashboards.

The system includes:

- **Sign Up:** Allows users to register with an email, password, and name. Upon account creation, a user profile is stored in Firestore.
- **Sign In:** Existing users can log in securely using their credentials.
- **Firestore Initialization:** On successful signup, two documents are created—users/{uid} and weights/{uid}—to store metadata and analytics-related fields, respectively.

Core Firebase functions used:

- createUserWithEmailAndPassword to register users
- signInWithEmailAndPassword to authenticate users
- updateProfile to assign the user’s display name
- setDoc to initialize Firestore documents for tracking app-specific metrics

No email verification or password reset features were implemented to maintain a lightweight user flow.

#### **Implementation Philosophy**

The goal of our authentication system was to provide **minimal friction** for users while ensuring we captured all necessary information to personalize their experience right after account creation. Here’s how we approached it:

- **Centralized Auth Service:** The Auth module exports two clean async methods: signUp() and signIn(). Both handle communication with Firebase Auth and Firestore, keeping business logic out of UI components and improving code modularity and testability.
- **Profile-First Onboarding:** Upon registration, we immediately populate the users collection with core metadata (uid, name, email, etc.) and the weights collection with fields relevant for the **smart credit scoring system** (paymentHistory, missedPayments, etc.). This structure ensures every authenticated user is onboarded with default values for downstream computations.
- **Error Handling and Validation:** Basic front-end validation checks ensure that no empty fields are submitted before initiating authentication. If validation fails, the function throws an error early. Errors thrown by Firebase are bubbled up and caught in the UI layer for display using snackbars or modals.
- **Display Name Handling:** After successful sign-up, we use updateProfile() to bind the display name (submitted during registration) to the Firebase user object. This name is later used in dashboards and expense group displays.

#### **Implementation Challenges**

During development of this authentication flow, we faced a few notable issues:

- **Missing Onboarding Detection:** Initially, the app lacked a reliable way to detect whether a newly signed-up user had completed onboarding. This led to users being routed to the dashboard with incomplete profiles. We resolved this by initializing Firestore documents immediately during signup (setDoc) with the required fields, ensuring a complete setup from the start.
- **Error Visibility for Empty Fields:** The first version of the code silently failed when any input field was left blank. To improve user feedback, we implemented a frontend check (if (email == "" || password == "" || name == "")) to catch these early and trigger a console error (extendable to UI errors).
- **Data Sync Between Auth and Firestore:** Firebase Authentication and Firestore operate asynchronously, so ensuring that the updateProfile() and both setDoc() operations completed reliably was important. We used await in sequence to make sure the user's metadata was fully available before proceeding to the next steps in the app.
- **Error Bubbling:** Since Firebase errors are generic (e.g., network failure, invalid email, user not found), we ensured that meaningful error messages were surfaced to the UI layer, although this can still be improved further with a dedicated error mapping utility.

## **10.1.5 Transfers & Top Ups**

## Description

This module handles key transactional features within OweNus:

- **Transfers:** Users can send virtual funds to other users by specifying a friend’s username and an amount. This updates the balances of both parties in real time and records the transaction in Firestore.
- **Top Ups:** Provides a way for users to simulate adding funds to their app balance, serving as an initial virtual currency mechanism for backend testing before WalletConnect integration was fully established.
- **Expenses:** Managed separately but integrated with transfers and balances to maintain accurate records of money owed and paid within groups.

The transfer feature also incorporates Web3 functionality by integrating WalletConnect to enable Ethereum wallet transactions that correspond to the app’s internal currency operations, adding a layer of real blockchain-based transfer.

### **Implementation Philosophy**

- **Separation of Concerns:** Each feature has its own screen and service layer. For example, TopUpScreen handles the top-up UI and calls TopUp.updateUserBalance(), while Transfer screen manages friend selection, amount input, and calls into TransferService for database operations and blockchain interaction.
- **Firestore for State & Records:** We use Firestore collections (users, transactions, balances) to maintain persistent state. Balances are tracked per user and per friend relationship to reflect debts owed or received. Transactions are logged in a separate collection for audit and history.
- **Atomic Balance Updates:** Transfers update balances for both sender and receiver, as well as mutual balance records, ensuring consistent data. Firestore increment operations guarantee atomic increments/decrements, minimizing race conditions.
- **Web3 Wallet Integration:** WalletConnect integration is leveraged for sending real ETH on the testnet to the receiver’s wallet address after internal balance updates, bridging the app’s virtual currency and blockchain.
- **User Experience:** Input validation (e.g., amount is numeric and present) and error alerts guide the user to prevent invalid transactions. UI feedback confirms transaction success or failure.

### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Synchronizing Off-Chain and On-Chain Balances:** Ensuring that Firestore balances remain consistent with on-chain Ethereum transactions requires careful sequencing. Currently, internal balance updates happen first, followed by the blockchain transaction. Failures in the blockchain transaction after database updates require robust error handling and user notification, which is still an area for future improvement.
- **Friend Name Lookup:** Transfers require searching for the friend by username in Firestore. This lookup must be optimized as it can be a bottleneck with large user bases. Currently, it assumes unique usernames and throws an error if not found.
- **Handling WalletConnect State:** The WalletConnect modal and provider lifecycle require managing connection status correctly, ensuring the user can connect, disconnect, and send transactions reliably.

- **Top Up as Temporary Feature:** The top-up screen was initially designed to test backend balance updating before WalletConnect was integrated. ### **Description**

The **Friends** screen enables users to build a social network within the app by adding other users as friends. Connections are established via email search. Once a friend is added, they automatically become selectable within the **Add Expense** screen, streamlining cost splitting between known contacts. This foundation also supports future features such as **group chats**, **leaderboards**, and **community wellness challenges**.

The screen offers:

### **Implementation Philosophy**

- **Bidirectional Friendships** Friendships are created in both directions for mutual discoverability and ease of expense sharing. When user A adds user B:

  - One friendships document is created with userId: A and friendId: B
  - A reciprocal document is created with userId: B and friendId: A

- This symmetrical design simplifies querying friend lists and ensures both users see the connection.
- **Email-Based Lookup** Friend search is based on email (not UID or username) to keep the process user-friendly. This choice also ensures a global uniqueness constraint, reducing the chance of misidentification.
- **Firestore Schema** Each friendship entry in the friendships collection includes:

  - userId (the one initiating or owning the friend list)
  - userName
  - friendId
  - friendName
  - createdAt

- **Duplicate & Self-Checks** Prevents:

  - Adding oneself as a friend
  - Adding the same friend multiple times (checked using friendEmails)
  - Adding non-existent users (verified using a lookup in the users collection)

- **UX Considerations**

  - A friendly, humorous empty state encourages social interaction
  - Clear input with placeholder guidance
  - Friend list auto-updates upon new addition
  - Gradient and images align with the app’s branding

### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Firestore Read Efficiency** Because friendships are stored as independent documents (not as arrays in user objects), each friend list retrieval requires a query to the friendships collection. This is scalable and flexible, but more expensive than single-document reads.
- **Email Uniqueness Assumption** The implementation assumes emails are unique identifiers across users — which is valid in Firebase Auth but requires consistency if users are manually added to the database.
- **Two-Way Document Creation** Adding friends involves two Firestore addDoc operations — one for each direction. This ensures symmetrical relationships but introduces potential consistency issues if only one write succeeds. A future improvement could use a **batched write** to guarantee atomicity.
- **No Realtime Sync (Yet)** Unlike other screens, the Friends screen uses a getDocs() call instead of onSnapshot(). As a result, newly added friends appear after adding, but future background updates (e.g. a friend removing you) wouldn’t be reflected without a refresh. Realtime syncing could be introduced later for consistency.

## **10.2.4 Expense Predictor (Machine Learning Integration)**

## **Description**

We developed a regression model to predict expense amounts based on contextual features such as the number of participants and the deadline month. This empowers users to budget proactively by estimating costs for hypothetical expense scenarios entered directly within the app.

### **Implementation Details**

**Data Pipeline and Preprocessing** The core dataset consists of historical expense records stored in Firestore. Using the Firebase Admin SDK within a Python environment, we fetched anonymized expense data for training. The main features extracted include:

- **Number of Participants:** Derived from the length of the participant list, capturing the effect of group size on expense magnitude.
- **Deadline Month:** Extracted from Firestore Timestamps to capture potential seasonal or monthly expense trends.
- **Amount:** The continuous target variable representing the actual expense value.

Strict anonymization was maintained by using only aggregate numeric features, ensuring no user-identifiable information was used in the training process.

**Feature Engineering and Scaling** The dataset was split into features (participants and deadline_month) and the target variable (amount). A train-test split was performed to validate model generalization. Features were normalized with a StandardScaler to have zero mean and unit variance, critical for stable and efficient neural network training.

**Model Architecture and Training** The model is a straightforward feedforward neural network built with Keras Sequential API, consisting of:

- An input layer matching the two features.
- Two hidden layers with 8 and 4 neurons respectively, using ReLU activation functions to capture nonlinear relationships.
- A single output neuron predicting the expense amount.

The model was compiled with the Adam optimizer, minimizing mean squared error (MSE) and tracking mean absolute error (MAE) for interpretability. Training ran for 50 epochs, achieving reasonable convergence given the limited dataset size.

**Model Export and Conversion** After training, the model was saved in Keras's .h5 format. This file was then converted to TensorFlow.js format using the official tensorflowjs_converter tool, producing a model.json file and binary weight shard files. These converted model assets were bundled with the React Native app.

**Client-Side Prediction with React Native** Within the React Native InsightsScreen, we utilized the @tensorflow/tfjs-react-native library to load the TensorFlow.js model locally on the device via the bundleResourceIO API. This enables fully offline inference, removing dependency on network connectivity.

At runtime, users input numeric values for participants and deadline month, which are validated and converted into tensors. The model performs synchronous inference and returns an immediate numeric prediction, which is displayed in an engaging UI featuring styled cards and icons.

### **Runtime and Static Model Nature**

The model used in the app is fundamentally **static at runtime**:

- Once loaded from the bundled assets, the model weights and parameters do not change dynamically.
- Predictions are generated locally on the device without backend calls, reducing latency and preserving privacy.
- This static deployment means the model cannot adapt or improve with new data or evolving user behaviors post-deployment.

**Advantages of this static approach include:**

- Simplicity in implementation and deployment.
- Fast, low-latency predictions even in offline scenarios.
- Strong privacy guarantees as user data never leaves the device.

**However, this approach also introduces limitations:**

- Model accuracy may degrade over time as expense patterns change.
- Lack of personalization since the model is generic and fixed.
- Updates require retraining the model offline and bundling new model files with app releases.

### **Challenges and Lessons Learned**

- **Complex Dependency Integration:** Integrating TensorFlow.js within an Expo-managed React Native environment proved challenging. It required installing expo-dev-client and configuring native dependencies such as expo-gl to support GPU acceleration and ensure compatibility across iOS and Android platforms.
- **End-to-End Pipeline Complexity:** Building a seamless pipeline—from Firestore data extraction to TensorFlow training, TensorFlow.js conversion, and finally React Native inference—required significant debugging and experimentation to ensure stability.
- **Model Accuracy Limitations:** The relatively small and imbalanced dataset limited prediction reliability. Future improvements depend on collecting larger and more diverse datasets, along with richer input features.
- **Privacy and Compliance:** By restricting features to aggregate numeric values and performing all inference locally, the system fully respects user privacy and complies with Firebase and academic data policies.

### **Future Improvements**

To overcome the static model’s limitations and enhance user experience, future work should focus on:

- **Cloud Deployment of the Model:** Hosting the TensorFlow model in the cloud to allow:

  - Dynamic, real-time updates without requiring app redeployment.
  - Continuous retraining and integration of new data for improved accuracy.
  - Scalable server-side inference leveraging more powerful compute resources.

- **Dynamic Model Updating:** Implementing over-the-air update pipelines to deliver new model versions automatically to users.
- **Personalization:** Exploring federated learning or hybrid client-cloud architectures to tailor predictions to individual users while maintaining data privacy.
- **Feature Expansion:** Incorporating additional features such as expense categories, user spending history, and external economic indicators to boost model performance and relevance.

## **10.3.2 Credibility Scoring System**

### **Overview**

To establish **trust and accountability** between users, we implemented a credibility scoring system that dynamically assigns a **credit score (0–100)** to each user. This score reflects the user’s past payment behavior, outstanding debts, and activity level within the app. It helps group members evaluate reliability and mitigate financial risks.

The system is backed by Firebase Firestore and runs entirely on the client side using real-time data fetched from the indivExpenses collection.

_Profile page to show credibility score (evidently I have not been paying my dues on time)_

### **Implementation Details**

#### **Data Inputs**

The system calculates the credit score using historical records of a user’s participation in shared expenses, specifically:

- status (paid or pending),
- createdAt (expense creation timestamp),
- deadline (payment deadline),
- amount (debt value).

All records are pulled from the indivExpenses collection where the user is a participant.

#### **Score Components**

Each user has a corresponding document in the weights Firestore collection, where the following indicators are computed and stored:

**Metric**

**Description**

paymentHistory

Percentage of expenses paid on time

avgSettlementTime

Average time taken (in days) to settle expenses

missedPayments

% of expenses overdue and still unpaid

totalAmountOwed

Total unpaid amount (normalized to a 0–100 scale)

activeExpenses

% of currently active (pending) expenses

totalExpenses

Total number of expense records for the user

Scoring Formula

The user’s credibility score is calculated using a **weighted scoring model**:

let score = totalExpenses == 0 ? 100 :

0.4 \* paymentHistory +

0.1 \* avgSettlementTime +

0.2 \* missedPayments +

0.15 \* totalAmountOwed +

0.1 \* activeExpenses +

0.05 \* totalExpenses;

- All components are normalized between 0–100 before weighting.
- The final score is clamped between 0–100 and stored in the users collection.
- A score of **100** is assigned by default to new users (with zero history).

#### **Status Interpretation**

Each numeric score is classified into a human-readable status:

**Score Range**

**Status**

80–100

Excellent

65–79

Good

50–64

Fair

0–49

Poor

These labels are displayed in the UI along with the numerical score to help users interpret credibility at a glance.

### **Runtime and Persistence**

- The credit score is recalculated whenever triggered manually (e.g. via a button press) or as part of any transaction-related event.
- The most recent score is stored in the users collection and can be used for group filtering, prioritization, or warnings.
- A custom version of the scorer (calculateCustomCreditScore) allows fetching scores for other users (e.g. by group leaders or friends).

Privacy Considerations

- Only aggregate and non-sensitive data (timestamps and expense amounts) are used.
- No personal information or descriptions are factored into scoring.
- All computations happen client-side using Firebase and are not shared externally.

### **10.4 Future Improvements**

**Future Improvements & Reflection**With the successful completion of Milestone 3, OweNus now offers a significantly more intelligent and user-centric experience, backed by blockchain-integrated transactions, real-time credit scoring, and a polished, consistent UI/UX across all major workflows. Among the highlights are the seamless MetaMask wallet connection that allows real-time reading of user balances, the Credibility Scoring system that introduces a trust layer into financial interactions, and various user experience upgrades that make navigating and using the app more intuitive.

As we look toward future development, there are several areas where the functionality of the app can be expanded further. The Credibility Scoring System—currently based on punctuality and consistency—can be enhanced with more granular metrics such as categorization of expenses (e.g., rent, food, utilities), user feedback or ratings within groups, and decay-based weighting where recent financial behavior plays a greater role in score computation. A visual dashboard showing the evolution of a user’s credit score over time can also motivate more responsible repayment behavior.

From a technical standpoint, two significant features remain on the roadmap: smart contract integration and AI model caching. While users can now initiate MetaMask-based transactions through the app, the lack of smart contracts limits the system’s ability to guarantee on-chain verification, automation, and immutability of fund transfers. Developing and deploying smart contracts would enable decentralized, trustless settlements, aligning OweNus more closely with modern decentralized finance (DeFi) systems. In parallel, implementing model caching for the AI-powered Expense Predictor—currently reloaded on every session—would reduce latency and significantly improve user experience when interacting with the Insights feature.

### **Reflection on Development Process**

One key learning from the development process was the architectural challenge introduced by the shift from a centralized database system to a blockchain-integrated one. In Milestones 1 and 2, the features we developed—such as expense splitting and peer-to-peer transfers—were closely tied to manually updating user balances in Firestore. These features relied heavily on backend logic that directly modified stored balance values. However, as we transitioned to blockchain-based operations in Milestone 3, this tightly coupled design presented a major hurdle. Since MetaMask reads wallet balances directly from the chain and cannot be updated via backend services, almost all backend logic tied to user balances had to be restructured or deprecated, leading to a significant re-engineering effort.

Looking back, a more effective strategy would have been to establish MetaMask connectivity and define our wallet interaction model from the beginning, then build features like transfers, expense splitting, and balance tracking on top of that foundation. By first anchoring the architecture around immutable, on-chain balances, we could have avoided redundant development and reduced the amount of refactoring required in later stages. This would have also allowed for earlier consideration of how smart contracts and blockchain interactions would influence user workflows, trust assumptions, and UI design.

Furthermore, time constraints played a role in some deferred implementations. More granular sprint planning, with buffer periods for integration-heavy features like smart contracts and model optimization, would have allowed us to better manage technical risks. Parallelizing feature development (e.g., one team focusing on AI improvements while another handled blockchain integration) could have maximized productivity within the same timeline.

Despite these challenges, Milestone 3 marks a strong leap forward in OweNus’ evolution. The core functionality is in place, and the architectural groundwork is now set for future enhancements. With planned improvements to credibility scoring, smart contract deployment, and performance optimizations, OweNus is well-positioned to become a robust, intelligent, and trustworthy financial platform for group expense management.
